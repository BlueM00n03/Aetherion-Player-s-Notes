
## Overview
The Kingdom of Thalmyr stands as the spiritual heart of the [[Imperium of Caelvar]], where faith, doctrine, and divine interpretation shape both society and governance.

In Thalmyr, the gods are not distant.

They are present, acknowledged, and followed with purpose.

---

## 🟢 Level 1 — Common Knowledge

### Identity
Thalmyr is known for its strong religious presence.

- Temples and sanctuaries are widespread  
- Clergy hold significant influence  
- Faith plays a central role in daily life  

---

### Reputation
To outsiders, Thalmyr is seen as:

- Devout  
- Ordered  
- Unwavering  

It is often considered the moral compass of the Imperium.

---

### Religion
Worship in Thalmyr is structured.

- People follow established pantheons  
- Faith is practiced openly  
- Religious teachings are widely respected  

---

### Role in the Imperium
Thalmyr provides:

- Spiritual guidance  
- Cultural cohesion  
- Moral framework for decision-making  

---

## 🟡 Level 2 — Educated Knowledge

### Pantheon Structure
Faith in Thalmyr is divided among recognized pantheons.

- Individuals devote themselves to one pantheon  
- Worship across pantheons is not commonly practiced  
- Religious identity is tied to belief systems, not just individual gods  

---

### Organized Faith
Religion in Thalmyr is not only personal—it is institutional.

- Religious orders maintain influence  
- Doctrine is preserved and interpreted  
- Disputes over belief are resolved within structured systems  

---

### Influence on the Imperium
Thalmyr’s influence extends beyond its borders.

- Religious leaders advise on major decisions  
- Faith can shape public opinion  
- Moral arguments often carry political weight  

---

### Relationship with Other Kingdoms
Thalmyr maintains ties with all kingdoms, though perspectives differ.

- Supports unity through shared belief  
- Sometimes clashes with more pragmatic or secular views  
- Seeks consistency in doctrine across the Imperium  

---

## Notes
In Thalmyr, belief is not only a matter of faith.

It is a matter of structure.
